Theorem 3.5 of the previous post tells recurrent and transient states apart through the series of the probabilities $p^{(n)}_{ii}.$ It is applied here to the study of random walks on $\mathbb{Z}^d.$

## Random walk on $\mathbb{Z}$

Let $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ be a Markov chain with set of states $\mathbb{Z}$ and transition matrix

$$
P = \begin{pmatrix}
\cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots \\
\cdots & q & 0 & p & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
\cdots & 0 & q & 0 & p & 0 & \cdots \\
\cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots
\end{pmatrix},
$$

where ${p, q \in (0, 1)}$ and ${p + q = 1,}$ as in Figure 1. The chain $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ is irreducible and all its states have period 2.

<WalkLine />

**Figure 1.** The random walk on ${\mathbb{Z}{:}}$ from each state the walk jumps to the right with probability $p$ and to the left with probability $q.$

We want to establish whether the states are recurrent or transient. For all ${n \ge 0}$ we compute

$$
p^{(2n)}_{00} = \binom{2n}{n} (pq)^n.
$$

Indeed, a path from 0 back to 0 in $2n$ steps makes $n$ steps to the right and $n$ to the left, in one of $\binom{2n}{n}$ orders, and each such path has probability ${(pq)^n.}$ By [Stirling's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling%27s_approximation)

$$
\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n^n \exp(-n) \sqrt{2\pi n}}{n!} = 1
$$

we find the asymptotic behaviour, as in Figure 2,

$$
p^{(2n)}_{00} \approx \frac{(4pq)^n}{\sqrt{\pi n}},
$$

where ${a_n \approx b_n}$ means that ${a_n / b_n \to 1.}$ Since ${0 \le 4pq = 4p(1 - p) \le 1,}$ with ${4pq = 1}$ if and only if ${p = q = 1/2,}$ and ${4pq < 1}$ otherwise, by Theorem 3.5 we deduce Proposition 4.1. Indeed, ${p^{(n)}_{00} = 0}$ for odd ${n,}$ so the series of Theorem 3.5 for the state 0 is the series of the ${p^{(2n)}_{00},}$ which diverges when ${4pq = 1}$ and converges when ${4pq < 1;}$ the chain is irreducible, so Corollary 3.6 extends the conclusion to all states.

**Proposition 4.1.** The following hold.\
**(i)** Symmetric random walk, ${p = q = 1/2{:}}$ all states are recurrent.\
**(ii)** Asymmetric random walk, ${p \neq q{:}}$ all states are transient.

**Figure 2.** The return probabilities $p^{(2n)}_{00}$ of the walk on $\mathbb{Z}$ for ${n = 1, \dots, 40,}$ dots, and their asymptotic value ${(4pq)^n/\sqrt{\pi n},}$ line. The slider sets ${p,}$ and ${q = 1 - p.}$

In the case ${p = q = 1/2}$ the first return times $T_i$ to a state $i$ are almost surely finite. However, by means of the generating functions $F_{00}$ and $P_{00}$ introduced in the proof of Theorem 3.5, we can show that their expectation $\mathbb{E}_i[T_i]$ is infinite. In other words, the return to $i$ is almost sure, but on average it occurs after a very long time.

Let us begin by recalling the Taylor series expansion

$$
(1 - 4x)^{-\frac12} = \sum_{n \ge 0} \binom{2n}{n} x^n, \qquad |x| < \frac14,
$$

which gives an explicit formula for the generating function ${P_{00}{:}}$

$$
P_{00}(s) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - 4pqs^2}}, \qquad |s| < 1.
$$

The generating function $F_{00}$ is

$$
F_{00}(s) = 1 - \big( P_{00}(s) \big)^{-1} = 1 - \sqrt{1 - 4pqs^2}, \qquad |s| < 1.
$$

By Abel's lemma, since ${4pq = 1,}$ we finally find

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_0[T_0] &= \sum_{n \ge 1} n f^{(n)}_{00} = \lim_{s \to 1^-} \sum_{n \ge 1} n f^{(n)}_{00} s^{n-1} \\
&= \lim_{s \to 1^-} \frac{dF_{00}(s)}{ds} = \lim_{s \to 1^-} \frac{4pqs}{\sqrt{1 - 4pqs^2}} = +\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$

Indeed, the first equality holds because $T_0$ is almost surely finite and ${\mathbb{P}_0\{ T_0 = n \} = f^{(n)}_{00}.}$ Lemma 3.4 applies to the power series with coefficients ${n f^{(n)}_{00} \le n,}$ whose radius of convergence is at least 1 and which is the derivative of $F_{00}$ for ${|s| < 1.}$

## Random walk on $\mathbb{Z}^2$

The return probabilities $p^{(2n)}_{00}$ of the walk on the plane are computed as those of the walk on $\mathbb{Z}.$

Consider a Markov chain $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ with set of states $\mathbb{Z}^2$ and transition probabilities

$$
\mathbb{P}\{ X_{n+1} = (i, j) \mid X_n = (h, k) \} = \begin{cases} 1/4 & \text{if } |i - h| + |j - k| = 1, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

The chain $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ is irreducible and has period 2. Let $P$ be the transition matrix; then

$$
p^{(2n)}_{00} = \sum_{h + k = n} \frac{(2n)!}{(h!)^2 (k!)^2} \Big( \frac14 \Big)^{2n},
$$

where 0 denotes ${(0, 0) \in \mathbb{Z}^2.}$ Simple computations yield

$$
\begin{aligned}
p^{(2n)}_{00} &= \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{(2n)!}{(k!)^2 ((n-k)!)^2} \Big( \frac14 \Big)^{2n} \\
&= \binom{2n}{n} \Big( \frac14 \Big)^{2n} \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} \binom{n}{n-k}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing the terms $a^n b^n$ in the binomial expansion of $(a + b)^{2n}$ and in the square of the binomial expansion of ${(a + b)^n,}$ we find the formula

$$
\binom{2n}{n} = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} \binom{n}{n-k}.
$$

Summing up,

$$
p^{(2n)}_{00} = \Big( \binom{2n}{n} \Big( \frac14 \Big)^n \Big)^2,
$$

and, by Stirling's formula, as in Figure 3,

$$
p^{(2n)}_{00} \approx \frac{1}{\pi n},
$$

which proves, by Theorem 3.5 and Corollary 3.6, that all states are recurrent.

## Random walk on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ in dimension three or more

The computation of $p^{(2n)}_{00}$ on $\mathbb{Z}^2$ extends to $\mathbb{Z}^d$ with ${d \ge 3.}$ The transition probabilities of the Markov chain $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ are now

$$
\mathbb{P}\{ X_{n+1} = (j_1, \dots, j_d) \mid X_n = (i_1, \dots, i_d) \} = \begin{cases} (2d)^{-1} & \text{if } \sum_{k=1}^{d} |i_k - j_k| = 1, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

In analogy with the random walks on $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}^2$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
p^{(2n)}_{00} &= \sum_{k_1 + \dots + k_d = n} \frac{(2n)!}{(k_1!)^2 \cdots (k_d!)^2} \Big( \frac{1}{2d} \Big)^{2n} \\
&= \binom{2n}{n} \Big( \frac{1}{2d} \Big)^{2n} \sum_{k_1 + \dots + k_d = n} \frac{(n!)^2}{(k_1!)^2 \cdots (k_d!)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last sum is dominated by

$$
\max_{k_1 + \dots + k_d = n} \Big\{ \frac{n!}{k_1! \cdots k_d!} \Big\} \sum_{k_1 + \dots + k_d = n} \frac{n!}{k_1! \cdots k_d!} = d^n \max_{k_1 + \dots + k_d = n} \Big\{ \frac{n!}{k_1! \cdots k_d!} \Big\},
$$

where we used an extension of the binomial formula, with ${x_1 = \dots = x_d = 1,}$

$$
\sum_{k_1 + \dots + k_d = n} \frac{n!}{k_1! \cdots k_d!} \, x_1^{k_1} \cdots x_d^{k_d} = (x_1 + \dots + x_d)^n.
$$

Elementary computations show that, when $d$ divides ${n,}$ the maximum value is attained for

$$
k_1 = k_2 = \dots = k_d = \frac{n}{d}.
$$

In detail, if ${k_a \ge k_b + 2}$ for some $a$ and ${b,}$ moving one unit from $k_a$ to $k_b$ multiplies ${n!/(k_1! \cdots k_d!)}$ by ${k_a/(k_b + 1) > 1,}$ so at the maximum the $k_i$ differ by at most 1, as in Figure 4. For the other $n$ the bound below holds with $(n/d)!$ read as ${\Gamma(n/d + 1),}$ since $\log \Gamma$ is convex and so ${k_1! \cdots k_d! \ge \Gamma(n/d + 1)^d}$ by Jensen's inequality.

<Multinomial client:visible />

**Figure 4.** An instance with ${d = 3{:}}$ the numbers ${n!/(k_1! \, k_2! \, k_3!)}$ over the triples with ${k_1 + k_2 + k_3 = n,}$ one hexagon each, darker for larger values, and the largest ones outlined. The slider sets $n.$

By Stirling's formula we find that $(p^{(2n)}_{00})_{n \ge 0}$ is asymptotically smaller than a constant times

$$
\binom{2n}{n} \Big( \frac{1}{2d} \Big)^{2n} \frac{n! \, d^n}{((n/d)!)^d} \approx \frac{d^{d/2}}{\pi^{d/2} 2^{(d-1)/2}} \frac{1}{n^{d/2}}.
$$

Since ${d \ge 3,}$ the states are transient, because ${\sum_{n \ge 0} p^{(2n)}_{00} < +\infty.}$ Figure 5 draws $p^{(2n)}_{00}$ for ${d = 1, 2, 3.}$
